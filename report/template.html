How I got here

Six steps, each written down before the next

The full log with every number is in LOG.md. The method was fixed before any 2026 odds were computed, so the answer couldn't steer it.

  1. Pulled every game since 2015

    About 29,000 regular-season and postseason results from the official MLB Stats API, plus the live 2026 bracket, rosters and announced starters. fetch_data.py

  2. Rated every team on runs, not wins

    A Poisson model gives each team an offense and a defense rating plus a home-field term, adjusted for opponents. A ridge penalty pulls ratings toward average, because 162 games overstate how far apart teams really are. model.py

  3. Tuned it on games it hadn't seen

    Fit through August, predict September, ten seasons over. The best setting was heavy shrinkage with no recency weighting: hot streaks did not predict. tune.py

  4. Graded it on 11 real Octobers

    440 playoff games and 113 series from 2015–2025, each predicted using only that year's regular season. backtest.py

  5. Ran the live bracket

    Each LCS from its current state, then all four possible World Series, with home field going to the better record. predict.py

  6. Tried to break it

    Nine alternative setups, a postseason-roster adjustment built from actual October starters, and a comparison against betting markets. sensitivity.py, roster_adjust.py

Can it be trusted?

The backtest: a real edge, a small one

The stronger team does not reliably win in October, so the useful question is whether the model's probabilities are honest. They are. When it called a series 55–60% for the favorite, the favorite won about 60% of the time. It beats a coin flip and beats "just use the standings," by modest margins.

Second opinion

Model vs the betting market

Sportsbooks agree on the favorite and on the order at the top. The real disagreement is the Dodgers: the market has them near 20%, and the model has 12%. My read is that the market prices in October deployment. When Skubal, Snell, Yamamoto and Glasnow start every game and Ohtani is in the mix, the Dodgers' staff projects at a 3.50 FIP instead of their 3.85 season mark. The roster-adjusted model moves to 16% for exactly that reason. I kept the pre-registered model as the headline and show the gap here rather than tuning toward the market.

Limits

What this model can't see

  • It rates teams on the full season. Injuries are only captured in the roster-adjusted scenario, which is not backtested.
  • It doesn't know tonight's specific matchup (for example, Peralta vs Messick) beyond the team-level rotation adjustment.
  • Bullpen fatigue, travel and day-to-day lineup choices are not modeled.
  • The backtest covers 113 series. That's enough to show the probabilities are honest, but not enough to fine-tune them.
  • Market odds came from published sportsbook prices on Oct 13 (SI, Fox Sports/DraftKings) and move by the hour.
